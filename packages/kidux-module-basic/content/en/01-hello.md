# Hello, computer

A computer does exactly what it is told, one thing after another, very
fast. A list of orders for it is called a **program**, and **BASIC** is a
language for writing programs. It is the language the first home
computers spoke, forty years ago, and it is still a fine way to start.

On the left of this window is your computer. At the top is the
**editor**, where you write. Under it are the buttons. Under them is the
**screen**, where your program shows what it does.

![The editor, the buttons and the screen](computer.svg)

> [point] Every line of a BASIC program starts with a number. Type these
> two lines in the editor, pressing Enter at the end of each one.

```basic
10 PRINT "HELLO"
20 PRINT "I AM A COMPUTER"
```

Now press **Run**. The screen says hello. Your first program works!

Each listing in this guide has a button, *Type it in for me*, which puts
it in the editor for you. Typing it yourself is better, though: that is
how your fingers learn where the keys are.

## Why 10, 20, 30?

The computer does the lines from the top of the editor to the bottom.
The number at the start of a line is its name: later you will meet
orders that jump to a line by its name. Two lines cannot have the same
name.

We number them 10, 20, 30 to leave room. If you want a new line between
line 10 and line 20, write it between them in the editor and call it 15.

> [think] Can you add a line 15 that says something about you?

## Keep trying

- Change the words between the quotes, and press Run again.
- Add a line 30 with a third sentence.
- Write `PRIMT` instead of `PRINT` on purpose, and press Run. The
  computer tells you it does not understand, and which line it is. Mend
  it and run it again: every programmer mends programs all day long.

::: adult
This guide teaches BASIC one idea per chapter, and each idea is tried on
the computer straight away. Let the child type the listings; the button
that types them is there for long ones and for tired hands. A child who
wants to change a program and see what happens is doing exactly the
right thing.

The BASIC here is wwwBASIC, a BASIC in the style of the PC's QBasic,
running in the browser. The guide is written for it. Its lines run in
the order they are written in the editor, and their numbers are their
names. Its letters are the PC's of the time, which had no accents.
:::
