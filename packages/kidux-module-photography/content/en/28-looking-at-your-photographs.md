# Looking at your photographs

A photograph is not finished when you press the button. Looking at it
afterwards, carefully, is how you find out what went right and what went
wrong, and how the next one gets better.

## Look closely

On the camera's small screen almost every picture looks sharp. It is not
true. **Zoom in**, on the eyes, on the thing that mattered, and look
again: is it sharp, or a little soft? Did the camera focus on the face,
or on the wall behind it?

## The numbers tell you what happened

Every picture remembers how it was taken: the mode, the time, the
aperture, ISO, the +/−. The camera can show them, with the picture's
histogram (chapter 5) beside it.

![A picture played back with all its numbers: P, 125, F5.6, +0.0, ISO 400, and its histogram](screen-playback-info.svg)

That is how you learn from a picture that went wrong:

- **Blurred?** Is everything blurred, even the wall? That is camera shake
  (chapter 4): look at the time, 1/30 or longer in the hand. Is only the
  moving thing blurred, and the rest sharp? That is movement (chapter 6).
- **Grainy?** Look at the ISO (chapter 8).
- **Too dark or too bright?** Look at the histogram, and at the +/−
  (chapter 5).
- **Only the background sharp?** The camera focused there (chapter 16).

> [think] A bad picture with its numbers teaches you more than a good one
> you do not understand.

## Keep, delete, protect

- **Delete** the pictures that are clearly bad: blurred, empty, the floor
  by mistake.
- **Keep** the ones you are not sure about, and look at them on a big
  screen. Many look better there, and some worse.
- **Protect** the very best, so that they cannot be deleted by accident.

And remember: the memory card fills up, and the battery runs down. Copy
the pictures off, and charge the battery, before the next day out.

::: try
1. Look at today's pictures, one by one, with all the numbers showing.
2. Find the one with the shortest time, and the one with the longest.
   What was different about them?
3. Zoom into a face. Are the eyes sharp?
4. Delete three pictures that are clearly bad, and protect the best.
:::

::: camera
The **playback** button, a triangle, shows the pictures; the sub dial goes
from one to the next, and the zoom lever shows several at once, towards
W, or magnifies one, towards T, up to 14 times (manual, p. 11 and 29).
**INFO** changes what is shown with the picture, as far as all its
numbers and the histogram (manual, p. 28). The **down** arrow erases the
picture shown, after asking (manual, p. 12), and **Fn1** protects it, so
that it cannot be erased (manual, p. 31). The USB cable
connects the camera to a computer, which sees its card as a disk
(manual, p. 76).
:::

## Keep trying

- Look at a picture you love. Read its numbers, and take another like it
  on purpose.
- Find your three blurriest pictures, and say why each one is blurred.
- Look at your pictures on a big screen with someone, and ask which one
  they would choose.

::: adult
This chapter teaches the habit of reviewing: checking sharpness by
magnifying, and reading the shooting data to understand a failure. It is
how the earlier chapters are put into practice.

Please copy the pictures off the card with the child regularly, into a
folder of their own, and keep a copy somewhere else too; a card is not a
safe place for the only copy.
:::
