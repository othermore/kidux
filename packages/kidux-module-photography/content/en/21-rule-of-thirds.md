# Where to put things: the rule of thirds

Where you put things in a picture is called **composition**, and here is
a trick of it that painters knew long before cameras. Cut the picture into
three, across and down, with two lines each way, like a noughts and
crosses board. The eye likes to find things **on those lines**, and best
of all **where they cross**. A thing in the very middle looks as if it
was just put there; the same thing on a third looks as if it belongs.

## Three things to try first

- **The horizon on a line, not in the middle.** On the bottom line when
  the sky is the beautiful part; on the top line when the land is.
- **Eyes on the top line.** In a portrait, put the eyes on the top third,
  not in the middle of the picture.
- **Room in front.** Something that moves or looks one way, a running dog,
  a face turned to the side, needs empty space **in front** of it, on the
  side it is going or looking, not behind it.

> [point] The camera can draw the lines on its screen for you. Turn them
> on, and after a week you will see them even with the camera off.

::: try
1. Switch on the grid on the camera's screen.
2. Find a tree or a lamp post. Take a picture with it in the very middle.
3. Take another with it on the left line, and one on the right line.
4. Take the sea, a field or the street with the horizon in the middle,
   then on the bottom line, then on the top line.
5. Ask a friend to look to the left. Put them on the right third, so that
   they look across the picture. Then on the left third, looking out of
   it. Which feels better?
:::

::: camera
The grid is called **Displayed Grid**, in the custom menu, among the
display settings (manual, p. 60). The shape of the picture can be
changed too, **4:3**, **16:9**, **3:2** or **1:1**, a square, in the
live control that OK opens (manual, p. 39).
:::

## Keep trying

- Look at the pictures in a book or a magazine. Find the horizon in each.
  How many are in the middle?
- Make a square picture, 1:1, of a flower in the middle. Does the
  middle work better in a square?
- Break the rule on purpose: a face right in the middle, looking straight
  at you. When does that feel strong?

::: adult
This chapter teaches the rule of thirds: subjects on the lines and their
intersections, the horizon off centre, eyes on the upper third, and room
in front of a moving or looking subject. It is a rule to learn and then
to break on purpose: symmetry, a centred face, a square frame all have
their place, and chapter 22 shows some.

The post pair is one photograph cropped twice, with the post in the
middle and on a third.
:::
