# Painting with light

In a dark room, with the shutter open for ten seconds, the camera sees
nothing at all, except light. Switch on a torch, point it at the camera,
and move it through the air: the torch **draws a line** in the picture,
everywhere it went. You, holding it in the dark, are not in the picture at
all, because you were never lit. This is called **light painting**.

![A circle drawn in the dark with a light, the shutter open all the while](light-painting-circle.jpg)

You can draw a star, a heart, a spiral, a house. You can write your name,
but it comes out backwards for the camera, so write it backwards, or read
it in a mirror afterwards. You can spin a torch on a string and draw a
whole ball of light.

![A ball of light painted on a jetty at night, by spinning a light round and round](light-painting-orb.jpg)

## Painting the room

The torch can do something else: light things. Hold it on a toy for a
second, from the side, and the toy appears in the picture, lit from that
side, with its shadow. Light a chair from the left, then a lamp from the
right, and the picture shows a room lit in a way no room ever was.

> [cheer] A light painting is the one photograph where you are the light,
> the brush and the painter all at once.

::: try
1. Make a room really dark, with the curtains shut and the lights off.
2. Put the camera on a table, pointing at an empty wall, with the
   self-timer on.
3. Set the time to **10 seconds**, the aperture to **F5.6** and ISO to
   **200**.
4. Press the button, walk in front of the camera with a torch pointing at
   it, and draw a big star in the air, slowly.
5. Look at the picture. Then try your name, a friend's name, and a ball on
   a string.
6. Last: put a toy on the table, and light it from the side for two
   seconds, while the shutter is open.
:::

::: camera
Use **M** (manual, p. 18): the control ring sets the time, **10"**, and
the sub dial the aperture. In the dark the camera cannot find anything to
focus on, so focus first with the room light on, on the place where you
will draw, then switch to **MF**, manual focus, so that it does not try
again in the dark (manual, p. 43): in MF, hold OK down for a moment, then
focus with the sub dial or the arrows. The self-timer gives you 12 seconds
to get into place (manual, p. 27). The camera stands still, so switch the
stabilizer off (manual, p. 36).
:::

## Keep trying

- Use torches of two colours, or put a sweet wrapper over the torch.
- Draw a frame round a friend standing still, and light their face with
  the torch for one second.
- Make the room darker, and the picture longer: 20 seconds, 30.

::: adult
Light painting is a long exposure in the dark with a moving light source:
the source draws, the person holding it, unlit, does not appear. It is
safe and spectacular with a torch or a phone's light. Candles and
sparklers need an adult, and sparklers belong outdoors; keep the dark room
clear of cables and toys to trip over.

Focusing in advance and switching to manual focus is the practical trick
that makes it work; autofocus cannot find anything in the dark.
:::
