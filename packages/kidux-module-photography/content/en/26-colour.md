# Colour, and no colour

## Colours that are friends

A picture with one **strong** colour against a calm one is easy to look
at: one red flower in a green field, a yellow coat against a grey wall.
Some colours look best together: put the colours round in a circle, the
**colour wheel**, and the ones opposite each other, **red and green**,
**blue and orange**, **yellow and purple**, make each other stronger.

![One red tulip in a sea of green leaves](complementary.jpg)

## Light has a colour too

A lamp's light is **orange**, much more than you notice; a shadow on a
sunny day is **blue**, lit by the blue sky. Your eyes and brain correct
it without telling you, so a white sheet of paper looks white anywhere.
The camera has to be told, and that is called **white balance**: you tell
it which white is white, and it corrects all the other colours with it.

::: compare
![A cup on white paper under a lamp, with the white balance set for daylight: everything orange](white-balance-lamp.jpg)

![The same, with the white balance set for the lamp: the paper is white again](white-balance-right.jpg)
:::

## No colour at all

A picture in **black and white** takes the colour away, and leaves the
light, the shadows and the shapes. Sometimes that is all a picture needs.
And there is a trick from the old days of film: a **red filter** makes a
blue sky dark, so that white clouds and buildings stand out against it.

::: compare
![Two houses in colour, under a blue sky](colour-houses.jpg)

![In black and white](monotone-houses.jpg)

![In black and white with a red filter: the blue sky goes dark](red-filter-houses.jpg)
:::

::: try
1. In a room lit only by a lamp, take a picture with white balance on
   **AUTO**, and again set for a lamp, the light bulb. Which is whiter?
2. Put a white sheet of paper under the lamp and do the one-touch white
   balance on it. Take the picture again.
3. Switch the picture mode to **Monotone**, and take a face, then a sky
   with clouds. Then add the red filter to the sky.
4. Outdoors in the sun, set the white balance for shade, and take a
   picture. What happened to the colours?
:::

::: camera
White balance is in the live control that OK opens: **AUTO**, or the
light you are in, sun, shade, cloud, lamp, fluorescent light, each with
its number in **kelvins** (manual, p. 38). **One-touch white balance**
measures a white sheet of paper (manual, p. 39). The picture mode
**Monotone**, also in the live control, makes black and white, and its
**B&W filter** setting can be yellow, orange, red or green (manual, p.
37 and 50). The **ART** position of the mode dial has the art filters
(manual, p. 19).
:::

## Keep trying

- Find a red thing against something green, and a blue thing against
  something orange.
- Set the white balance to the lamp, and go outside. What colour is
  everything now?
- Take a portrait in Monotone. Do the eyes stand out more?

::: adult
This chapter covers colour harmony (complementary colours), the colour
of light and white balance, and black and white with a red filter. In a
sentence for a curious child: the colour of light is measured in
kelvins, low for the orange of a candle or a lamp, high for the blue of
shade.

The white-balance pair is one photograph of a cup on white paper, its
colours changed as a lamp's light with daylight white balance changes
them; the black-and-white pictures are one colour photograph made grey
from all its colours, and from its red alone, as a red filter does. The
art filters are fun; the course comes back to plain pictures, where the
light does the work.
:::
