# People and moments

A photograph of a person is about **one moment**: the second before
they laugh, the second they jump, the look when they open a present. The
moment does not wait.

## Wait for it, then take many

- **Be ready before it happens.** The camera on, the settings made,
  focused, framed, half-way down. Then wait.
- **Take several, one after another.** A person blinks, turns, laughs.
  Out of five pictures in a row, one is the one. Cameras can take many
  pictures a second while you hold the button down.
- **Posed or not.** A friend smiling at the camera is one kind of
  picture; a friend busy with something who has forgotten the camera is
  another, and often the better one.

![A horse jumping a fence, four pictures in a row: the third, at the top of the jump, is the one](burst-strip.jpg)

## Faces

- **The eyes must be sharp**, above everything else. Focus on the eye
  nearest the camera.
- **Eyes on the top third** (chapter 21).
- **Not from below the chin.** Hold the camera at their eyes, or a little
  above.

![A portrait: the eyes sharp, on the top third](portrait-eyes.jpg "thirds")

> [think] Ask before you photograph someone, and stop when they say so.
> A picture is never worth making someone unhappy.

::: try
1. Ask a friend to tell you a joke. Hold the button down and take a row
   of pictures while they tell it. Choose the one.
2. Take a portrait with the eyes on the top line of the grid, focused on
   the near eye.
3. Photograph someone busy: cooking, drawing, reading. Wait until they
   forget you.
:::

::: camera
Press the **down** arrow for the self-timer and sequential shooting:
sequential takes about **5** pictures a second, and the fast setting about
**15**, while you hold the button down (manual, p. 27). **Face priority**
and **eye priority** are in the live control (manual, p. 44), and the
picture mode **Portrait** is made for skin (manual, p. 37).
:::

## Keep trying

- At a birthday, photograph the moment the candles are blown out.
- Take your family at dinner, without asking them to smile. Then asking.
- Take hands: hands cooking, hands drawing, hands playing.

::: adult
This chapter is about timing and people: anticipating, shooting in
bursts and choosing, candid and posed pictures, sharp eyes, and
camera height for faces. Above all, it teaches asking permission and
stopping when asked.

Please talk with the child about photographs of other people, and above
all of other children: they are taken only with permission, and not
shared without their parents' agreement. The guide's own photographs
show no child's face.
:::
