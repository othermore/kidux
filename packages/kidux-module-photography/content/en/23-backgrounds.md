# Backgrounds, and tidying up

When you look at a friend, you see your friend. The camera sees
**everything**: the lamp post behind them, the bin, the washing on the
line, the mess on the table. And in the picture, the lamp post seems to
grow out of their head.

::: compare
![A lamp post right behind a friend, growing out of her head](pole-behind.jpg)

![Three steps to the side: the post has moved to the edge](pole-aside.jpg)

## Three ways to tidy

- **Move.** Three steps to the left or the right, and the thing behind
  moves too, out of the way. It is almost always the answer.
- **Change height.** Kneel, and the background becomes sky. Climb up,
  and it becomes grass or the floor.
- **Come closer.** Fill the whole picture with what matters, and there is
  no room left for anything else. A wide aperture helps, by blurring what
  is left (chapter 18).

> [point] Before you press, look at the **edges** of the screen, and
> behind the thing, not at the thing. That is where the surprises are.

## Empty on purpose

The opposite also works: lots of **empty space** round one small thing,
the sky, a white wall, fog. Then the small thing is the whole picture,
because there is nothing else to look at.

![The sun, a small white disc, in a great grey sky of fog](negative-space.jpg)

::: try
1. Take a portrait of a friend wherever they are standing, without
   thinking.
2. Look at it, and find the ugliest thing behind them.
3. Move until that thing is gone: to the side, lower, higher. Take it
   again.
4. Now come so close that only their face fits in the picture.
5. Last, find one small thing, a leaf, a stone, a toy, and put it alone
   on a big empty floor or against the sky.
:::

::: camera
Nothing to set here either: every camera sees the whole background. The
tilting screen (manual, p. 3) makes it easy to hold the camera low or
high, and **A** at the widest aperture blurs what is left (chapter 18).
:::

## Keep trying

- Take a picture of your room as it is. Tidy one corner, and take it
  again. Which would you show?
- Photograph a toy on a sheet of white paper, curved up behind it, so
  that the background disappears.
- Look through your pictures: how many have a lamp post or a tree growing
  out of someone's head?

::: adult
This chapter teaches the habit that improves children's photographs the
most: checking the background and the edges before pressing, and moving
to clean them up. Negative space, much empty area round a small subject,
is the same idea used the other way. The two pictures of the
portrait, before and after a step to the side, are made by a computer;
the woman in them is nobody real.
:::
