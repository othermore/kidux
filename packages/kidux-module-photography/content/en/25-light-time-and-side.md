# Light has a time and a side

A photograph is made of light, and light is not always the same. It has
a **time of day**, and it comes from a **side**.

## The time

- **An hour after sunrise and before sunset** the sun is low, and its
  light is warm, golden and soft, with long shadows. Photographers call it
  the **golden hour**, and many wait all day for it.
- **At noon**, in summer, the sun is high above. Its light is white and
  hard, and shadows are short and black: under a hat, under the eyes, a
  face gets two dark holes.
- **On a cloudy day** the whole sky is one giant soft lamp. No hard
  shadows at all: lovely for faces and flowers.

![Dogs running in a field an hour before sunset: warm, low light and long shadows](golden-hour.jpg)

## The side

Where the light comes from, compared with the camera, changes the shape
of everything.

![A ball lit from the front, flat; from the side, round; from behind, dark with a bright rim](light-sides.svg)

- **From the front**, from behind the camera: everything is lit, and
  everything looks **flat**.
- **From the side**: one half bright, one half in shadow, and things look
  **round**, with their bumps and shape.
- **From behind**: a bright **rim** round the edge, a halo in the hair;
  or, when you measure the bright sky, a black shape with no detail, a
  **silhouette**.

> [oops] Never look at the sun through the camera, and never point the
> lens at the sun for long. The sun can hurt your eyes and the camera.

::: try
1. Sit a friend by a window. Take their face with the window behind you,
   then beside them, then behind them.
2. Take the same corner of the garden or the street at noon, and again
   an hour before sunset.
3. Ask a friend to stand against the sunset. Turn the +/− to **−1** or
   **−2**, and take them as a black shape.
4. On a cloudy day, take a flower. Then take it on a sunny day.
:::

::: camera
For a silhouette, turn the sub dial to **−1** or **−2**, the exposure
compensation of chapter 5 (manual, p. 23). For a face against the light,
the other way round: measure a spot on the face (chapter 10), or light it
with the fill-in flash (chapter 15). The camera's own safety notes say
not to point it at the sun (manual, p. 94 and 95).
:::

## Keep trying

- Find shadows that make a picture by themselves: a fence's stripes on
  the ground, your own long shadow at sunset.
- Light a toy with a torch from below, in a dark room. What does it look
  like?
- Take the same tree every hour of one day.

::: adult
This chapter covers the quality and direction of light: golden hour,
hard midday light, soft overcast light; front light flattens, side light
models, back light rims or silhouettes. The golden-hour photograph has no
noon partner of the same place; the experiment in *Try it* gives the
child their own pair.

Please make sure the child never looks at the sun through the camera or
leaves the lens pointed at it; the camera's safety notes say the same
(p. 94 and 95).
:::
