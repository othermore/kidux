# The camera's modes: who chooses

The dial with letters on top of a camera answers one question: **who
chooses the time and the aperture, you or the camera?** Each letter is a
different answer.

![The XZ-2's mode dial: iAUTO, P, A, S, M, C1, C2, SCN, ART](mode-dial.svg)

- **AUTO** (on some cameras **iAUTO**, or a green camera): the camera
  chooses everything, the time, the aperture, ISO, the flash. You frame and
  press. Many cameras let you tell it in words what you want, *"blur the
  background"*, *"show the movement"*, and it works out how.
- **SCN**, scenes: you tell the camera what you are photographing, a
  portrait, sport, a sunset, fireworks, and it chooses what suits it.
- **P**, program: the camera chooses the pair of time and aperture, and you
  may shift it (chapter 9), and choose ISO, the flash and the +/−.
- **A**, aperture priority: **you choose the aperture**, how much is
  sharp, and the camera chooses the time to make it bright enough. On some
  cameras it is called **Av**.
- **S**, shutter priority: **you choose the time**, freeze or blur, and
  the camera chooses the aperture. On some cameras it is called **Tv**.
- **M**, manual: **you choose both**, and the camera only tells you what
  it measured. Chapter 12 is all about it.

## Which one?

- When **what is sharp** matters most, a face with the background melted,
  or a landscape sharp from near to far: **A**.
- When **movement** matters most, a jump frozen, a waterfall turned to
  silk: **S**.
- When the light **holds still** and you want every picture alike: **M**.
- When you have no time to think, or are just starting: **AUTO** or **P**.

> [cheer] A photographer who understands A and S understands the whole of
> exposure. You are nearly there.

::: try
1. Find something that moves over and over: a swing, a tap, a fan.
2. Take it in AUTO. Look at the time and the aperture the camera chose.
3. Take it in **A** at the widest aperture.
4. Take it in **S** at 1/1000, and again at 1/8.
5. Put the four side by side. Which one shows the movement best? Which
   one has the softest background?
:::

::: camera
The XZ-2's mode dial has **iAUTO**, **P**, **A**, **S**, **M**, **C1**,
**C2**, **SCN** and **ART** (manual, p. 8). In A the control ring sets the
aperture, in S the time, and in both the sub dial sets the exposure
compensation; in M the control ring sets the time and the sub dial the
aperture (manual, p. 16 to 18). The rings can be given other jobs in the
menus (manual, p. 89).

In iAUTO, OK opens the **live guide**: a list down the side of the screen,
change the colour, the brightness, blur the background, show the movement,
with a slider for each, and tips for shooting (manual, p. 22).

![The live guide in iAUTO: what can be asked for down the right, and the slider of the one chosen](screen-live-guide.svg)

**SCN** has scenes such as portrait, landscape, sport, night scene, sunset,
fireworks and beach and snow (manual, p. 20), and **ART** has filters such
as pin hole and diorama (manual, p. 19). **C1** and **C2** call back
settings you saved yourself (manual, p. 48).
:::

## Keep trying

- Take a portrait of a friend in SCN's portrait scene and in A at the
  widest aperture. Did the camera choose the same aperture?
- Take a picture in ART with the pin hole filter. Which chapter does it
  remind you of?
- Find the mode dial on another camera, or the "pro" mode on a phone.
  Which letters does it have?

::: adult
This chapter explains the exposure modes by who chooses the time and the
aperture: fully automatic, scenes, program, aperture priority, shutter
priority and manual, and when each suits. Other makers call A and S "Av"
and "Tv"; phones often hide them under a "pro" mode.

The four screens are drawn after the XZ-2's; in the drawings, and not on
the camera itself, the value the child chooses in each mode is framed, so
that the four can be told apart at a glance.
:::
