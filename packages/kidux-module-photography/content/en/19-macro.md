# Very close: macro

Get the camera very close to something small, a coin, a leaf, a bit of
cloth, and the whole picture fills with it. A coin becomes a landscape of
hills and letters; a leaf has roads and rivers in it, its veins. Taking
pictures that close is called **macro**.

![A coin from very close: every scratch shows](macro-coin.jpg)

![A leaf from very close: the veins, like roads on a map](macro-leaf.jpg)

## What changes close up

- **The sharp slice is paper-thin.** Up close, the depth of field of
  chapter 18 is less than a millimetre. Move the camera a hair after it
  has focused, and the picture is soft. Hold it on a table, or rest your
  elbows.
- **Your shadow is in the way.** The camera is so close that it, and you,
  shade the thing you are taking. Turn round until the light comes from
  the side.
- **Light from the side shows the bumps.** Light from straight in front
  makes a coin flat. Light from the side, low, makes every scratch and
  letter throw a little shadow, and stand up.

::: compare
![A coin lit from the front: flat, its ship hard to see](coin-front-light.jpg)

![The same coin lit from the side, low: every line of the ship stands up](coin-side-light.jpg)
:::

> [point] Close up, move the toy, not the camera: it is easier to slide a
> coin a little than to hold a camera still a centimetre from it.

::: try
1. Switch the camera to **super macro**.
2. Put a coin on a table by a window, and take it from as close as the
   camera lets you.
3. Take the same coin with a torch shining on it from the front, and
   again from the side, very low. Which shows the letters better?
4. Find a leaf, a piece of cloth, a flower, the skin of an orange, a
   sponge. Take each from very close.
5. Show the pictures to someone, and ask what they are.
:::

::: camera
Press **OK** for the live control, go down to the AF mode, and choose
**super macro**: the XZ-2 then focuses from just **1 cm** in front of the
lens, up to 60 cm (manual, p. 92). In super macro the camera sets the
zoom itself, and neither the zoom nor the flash can be used (manual, p.
43). Without super macro, the camera focuses from 5
cm away at the wide end of the zoom (manual, p. 92).
:::

## Keep trying

- Take your own fingertip. Can you see the lines of your fingerprint?
- Take a drop of water on a leaf after the rain.
- Make a guessing game: ten things from the house, so close that nobody
  knows what they are.

::: adult
This chapter covers close-up photography: very thin depth of field, the
need for a steady camera, the camera's own shadow, and side light to show
texture.

Insects are photographed where they are, never caught or moved for the
picture. A lens one centimetre from a leaf needs the light coming past
the child, not from behind the camera; a window to the side is ideal.

The coin pair is made by a computer, and so is its coin, which is no
real money: the same scene with only the light moved.
:::
