# The flash: light that freezes

A camera's flash is a burst of light so short, a thousandth of a
second or less, that whatever it lights is **frozen**, even when
the shutter is open much longer. Water splashing into a glass, too fast
for any eye, stops in the air, every drop sharp.

![A toy fish dropped into a glass of water, lit by a flash: the splash is frozen in the air](flash-splash.jpg)

## What a flash is good for, and what not

- **Freezing**: a splash, a jump, a pillow fight, in a dim room.
- **Filling in**: outdoors, with the sun behind a friend, their face is in
  shadow. A little flash lights the face, and the sunny background stays
  as it was. It is called **fill-in flash**.
- **Not far**: a small flash reaches a few metres. At a concert, from the
  back of the hall, it lights the heads in front of you and nothing else.
- **Not soft**: it makes hard shadows behind people, shiny faces, and red
  eyes, because it lights the inside of the eye.

![A cat in front of a bright window, without flash and with fill-in flash](fill-flash-pair.jpg)

## Flash and a long time together

With a long time and the flash, both happen in one picture: the long time
draws the moving light, and the flash freezes the thing once. When the
flash fires at the start, the thing is sharp where it began, and its trail
runs on ahead as if it went backwards. When the flash fires at the end,
just before the shutter closes, the trail is behind it, sharp where it
finished, and it looks as if it rushed forward.

![A rolling ball at a one-second picture with the flash. Above, the flash at the start: the trail runs ahead. Below, the flash at the end: the trail is behind, as it should be](flash-sync.svg)

> [oops] Never fire a flash close to someone's eyes, and never at a baby.
> Stand at least a metre away.

::: try
1. Put a glass of water on a table in a dim room, the camera on the table.
2. Raise the flash, and drop a small toy or a spoonful of water into the
   glass while you press the button. It takes a few tries.
3. Outdoors, with the sun behind a friend, take a picture without flash
   and one with fill-in flash.
4. In a dim room, set a time of **1 second** with the flash, and ask a
   friend to walk across, holding a torch. Try the flash at the start and
   at the end.
:::

::: camera
Slide the **flash switch** to raise the flash, then press the **right**
arrow, which has a lightning bolt, to choose how it fires: **AUTO** in the
dark or against the light; **red-eye reduction**, which flashes a few times
first; **fill-in**, always; **off**; and **slow synchronization**, which
keeps a long time to light the background too (manual, p. 24). Not every
mode has every one: slow synchronization is in P and A (manual, p. 25).

**# Sync** in the second shooting menu fires the flash at the start, Sync
1, or at the end, Sync 2 (manual, p. 52). Too close, the flash is too
bright even at its weakest (manual, p. 25); its strength can be turned
down in the live control (manual, p. 41). Keep it at least a metre from
anyone's face (manual, p. 94).
:::

## Keep trying

- Take a picture of a friend jumping, in a dim room, with and without the
  flash.
- Take a picture with the flash of something shiny, a window or a mirror.
  What does the flash do?
- Hold a white sheet of paper in front of the flash, leaning back like a
  slide, so that its light is thrown up at the ceiling. In a room with a
  white ceiling, are the shadows softer?

::: adult
This chapter covers the flash's short duration, which freezes motion; its
limited range; fill-in flash against the light; red-eye; and the two
synchronisations with a slow shutter (first and second curtain).

Please supervise the flash: never close to eyes, never at infants, at
least a metre away, as the camera's safety notes say. The water-splash
experiment is messy and fun; a towel helps. The cat pair is one photograph
of a cat by a window, without and with fill-in flash. The two pictures of
the walk with the torch are made by a computer, the same second with the
flash at its start and at its end.
:::
