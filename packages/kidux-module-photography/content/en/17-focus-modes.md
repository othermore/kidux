# Focus modes: once, following, by hand

Pressing the button half-way makes the camera focus. But what should it
do next, while you hold it? That is what the **focus mode** decides.

- **Once.** The camera focuses once, beeps, and keeps that distance for
  as long as you hold the button. It is right for anything that stands
  still: a toy, a house, a friend posing. It is the one to leave on, and
  it is what makes *focus, then frame* work. Its short name is **S-AF**,
  single autofocus.
- **Following.** The camera keeps focusing all the time you hold the
  button, again and again, so a dog running towards you stays sharp
  while it comes closer. Its short name is **C-AF**, continuous
  autofocus.
- **Following one thing.** The camera locks on to one thing and its
  square moves across the screen after it, wherever it goes. It is called
  **tracking**.
- **By hand.** The camera does not focus at all: **you** turn the focus,
  near or far, and watch the screen until the thing is sharp. Its short
  name is **MF**, manual focus.

> [point] For a friend running towards you, *once* focuses where they
> were when you pressed, and by the time the picture is taken they are
> closer, and soft. *Following* keeps up.

## When to focus by hand

Focusing by hand is slower, but it never makes a mistake of its own:

- in the **dark**, where the camera cannot see anything to focus on, as
  for painting with light (chapter 14);
- through a **window**, where the camera focuses on the glass, or on a
  drop of rain on it;
- for a picture taken **over and over** from the same place, the same
  distance every time;
- for something **very small** in a big picture, a bird on a branch,
  where the camera keeps choosing the branch.

While you focus by hand, the camera can **magnify** the
picture on the screen, so that you can see when it is sharp, and it
shows the distance it is focused on.

![Focusing by hand: the picture magnified, and the distance focused on at the bottom](screen-mf.svg)

::: try
1. Set the camera to **A** at **F1.8**, and zoom all the way in.
2. Ask a friend to walk slowly towards the camera from ten steps away. In
   **S-AF**, press half-way when they are far, hold it, and press the rest
   of the way when they are two steps away. Are they sharp?
3. In **C-AF**, do the same. Are they sharp now?
4. Tie a toy to a string, and pull it across the floor in **C-AF+TR**.
   Does the square follow it?
5. Put a toy behind a window pane, and press half-way in S-AF. Where did
   it focus? Now focus by hand, in **MF**, until the toy is sharp.
:::

::: camera
Press **OK** for the live control, and go down to the **AF mode** row:
**S-AF**, once; **super macro**, for very close things (chapter 19);
**C-AF**, following; **C-AF+TR**, following one thing; and **MF**, by
hand (manual, p. 43).

In MF, hold **OK** down for a moment, then turn the focus with the sub
dial or the up and down arrows (manual, p. 43). With the **lever**
beside the lens switched on, the control ring focuses instead (manual,
p. 43 and 59). **MF assist**, once an adult switches it on in the menus,
magnifies the picture while the control ring focuses (manual, p. 59),
and the distance can be shown in metres or in feet (manual, p. 64).
:::

## Keep trying

- In C-AF, take a picture of a ball rolling towards you. And in S-AF.
- Focus by hand on a toy, then move it a little closer without touching
  the focus. How far can it move before it is soft?
- Find a spider's web or a fence in front of the garden. Which way of
  focusing makes the web sharp, and which the garden?

::: adult
This chapter covers the focus modes: single AF, which locks the focus
with the half-press and suits still subjects; continuous AF and tracking,
for subjects that move, especially towards the camera; and manual focus,
for darkness, glass, repeated framing and small subjects in clutter.

For sport and running animals, C-AF keeps up with the subject for each
picture. On the XZ-2, sequential shooting, which takes pictures for as
long as the button is held, works only in S-AF and MF and keeps the
focus of the first picture (manual, p. 27): for a runner coming closer,
single pictures in C-AF are the better way.

The two pictures of the dog are made by a computer, the same moment with
only the focus changed.
:::
