# Zoom: near and far, and what it does to faces

The **zoom** makes the lens see a wider or a narrower part of the world.
Zoomed out, at **wide**, it takes in a big room. Zoomed in, at **tele**,
it sees only a small piece of it, as big as the whole picture, as if it
were a telescope.

The zoom's position is given as a length in millimetres: a small number
is wide, a big one is long, or tele.

## It is not only nearer

Here is the surprise. Take a toy from close up with the zoom at wide, and
then walk back and zoom in until the toy is the same size again. The toy
looks the same; **the background does not**. At wide, the house behind
is small and far away. Zoomed in, from further back, the same house looms
up right behind the toy.

It happens to faces too. From very close, at wide, the nose is much
nearer the camera than the ears, so it comes out big, and the ears small:
a funny face. From a few metres back, zoomed in, nose and ears are almost
the same distance away, and the face looks as it really is.

> [think] For a portrait, step back and zoom in. Never take a friend's
> face at arm's length at wide, unless you want to make them laugh.

## The zoom that is only cutting

Some cameras can zoom further than their lens, by **digital zoom**: they
only cut out the middle of the picture and blow it up. It is just like
cutting a photograph smaller and enlarging it afterwards, and it makes
nothing sharper. Moving closer is always better, when you can.

::: try
1. Ask a friend to sit still, in front of a wall or a window.
2. Zoom out all the way, and get close until their face fills the
   picture. Take it.
3. Walk back about three metres, zoom in until their face fills the
   picture again, and take it.
4. Compare the two noses, and the two backgrounds.
5. Do the same with a toy in front of a house across the street.
:::

::: camera
The **zoom lever** round the shutter button zooms: towards **W** for
wide, and towards **T** for tele (manual, p. 10). The lens goes from 28
to 112 mm, the lengths a large camera would need for the same view
(manual, p. 92). The **digital tele-converter** in the menus doubles the
zoom by cutting the picture, as the chapter says (manual, p. 65).
:::

## Keep trying

- Take the same street at wide and zoomed in. Which one makes the cars
  look closer together?
- Look at the moon at tele. How big is it on the screen?
- "Zoom with your feet": take a picture without touching the zoom, only
  walking closer and further. When is that better, and when impossible?

::: adult
This chapter separates the two things a zoom does: it changes the field
of view, and, because the photographer moves to keep the subject the
same size, it changes the perspective: the relative size of near and far
things depends only on where the camera stands. That is why portraits
are taken from a few metres at a longer focal length, which flatters a
face, and why a wide angle close up exaggerates a nose.

"Zoom with your feet" is good advice for composition, and impossible
for a bird or a stage. Digital zoom only crops; the XZ-2's tele-converter
is the same thing done in the camera.
:::
