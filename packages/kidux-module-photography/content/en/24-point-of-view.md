# Point of view

Most photographs in the world are taken from the same place: standing up,
the camera in front of a grown-up's eyes. That is why they look alike.
Change the **height** of the camera, and the same thing becomes a new
picture.

![A dog photographed from three heights: from high up, looking down; from a grown-up's eyes; from the floor, at the dog's own eyes](heights.svg)

- **Down low**, on your knees or lying on the floor, things look big and
  important, and a small dog becomes a giant. It is called the **worm's
  view**.
- **At their own height**: a dog, a cat or a little brother looks best
  taken from the height of **their** eyes, not from above.
- **Up high**, on a chair or a step, you look down on a table, a game, a
  pattern on the floor: the **bird's view**.

![From the ground: a dog walks past, huge, over the grass](worm-view.jpg)

:::

> [cheer] Knees and elbows get dirty in good photographs. That is how you
> can tell.

::: try
1. Put a shoe on the floor.
2. Take it from five heights: standing on a chair, standing, kneeling,
   sitting, lying with the camera on the floor.
3. Put a toy on the floor, and the camera on the floor too, right in front
   of it, so that the room behind looks enormous.
4. Photograph the breakfast table from straight above, standing on a
   chair, with an adult holding it steady.
:::

::: camera
The XZ-2's screen **tilts** up and down (manual, p. 3), so you can put
the camera on the floor and look down at the screen, or hold it above
your head and look up at it. Many cameras have such a screen; on a phone,
turn it upside down to get it closer to the ground.
:::

## Keep trying

- Photograph a friend from below and from above. Which makes them look
  tall and strong?
- Lie under a tree and take the branches against the sky.
- Take a toy car from its own height, as if you were another car.

::: adult
Point of view changes a photograph more than any setting: low angles
make a subject imposing, high ones show patterns and layouts, and
photographing children and animals at their eye level respects them.
Expect the child to lie on the ground and climb on chairs; a hand on the
chair is a good idea.
:::
