# What a camera is made of

You know the two oldest parts of a camera now: the dark box and the lens.
Here are all of them, in the order the light meets them.

![A camera cut open, the light coming in from the left](camera-parts.svg)

1. **The lens**, in blue in the drawing: the glass that bends the light to
   a sharp picture.
2. **The aperture**, the dark blades in the middle of the lens: a hole
   that opens wide and closes small, like the black middle of your eye,
   which grows in the dark and shrinks in the sun. It decides how much
   light comes in at once. Chapter 7 is all about it.
3. **The shutter**, the orange door near the back: it stays shut, and
   opens for a moment when you take the picture. It decides for how long
   the light comes in. Chapter 6 is about it.
4. **The sensor**, the little squares: the wall at the back of the dark
   room, made of millions of tiny squares that each count how much light
   landed on them, and what colour it was. Each square is one dot of your
   photograph, a **pixel**.
5. **The screen**, at the back: it shows you what the sensor sees, before
   and after the picture.
6. **The memory card**, in blue at the bottom: where the pictures are
   kept, thousands of them, even when the camera is off.

> [think] The aperture says *how much* light, the shutter says *for how
> long*. Can you guess why a camera needs both? Chapter 9 answers it.

## Finding your way on any camera

Every camera has these parts, but each maker puts the buttons in a
different place and gives them different names. Here is how to find what
you need on any camera:

- **A dial with letters**, usually on top: AUTO, P, A, S, M, and a few
  pictures. It chooses who decides, you or the camera (chapter 11).
- **A wheel or a ring** that turns, near your thumb or round the lens: it
  changes the numbers.
- **A button with a lightning bolt**: the flash.
- **A button with a flower**: for very close pictures (chapter 19).
- **A button with a triangle**, like the play button of a music player:
  it shows the pictures you took.
- **MENU**: everything else, in lists.

When you cannot find something, look for its picture on a button, then in
the MENU, and if it is not there, ask an adult to look in the camera's
manual with you.

::: try
1. Ask an adult to help you charge the battery and put it and the memory
   card in the camera.
2. Find these five things on your own camera: the lens, the
   screen, the shutter button, the dial with letters and the card's door.
3. Switch the camera on. Set the dial to AUTO, the fully automatic mode.
4. Point the camera at something you like, press the shutter button all
   the way down, and let go.
5. Press the button with the triangle and look at your first photograph.
:::

::: camera
![The Olympus XZ-2 from the front, with its lens cap beside it](xz2-front.jpg)

On the XZ-2 the parts are where the drawings below show them (manual,
p. 2 and 3). On top, from left to right: the flash's housing, which pops up
with the **flash switch** at the back; the **hot shoe** for an extra flash;
the small **ON/OFF** button; the **shutter button** with the **zoom lever**
round it; and the **mode dial** with its letters at the right end.

![The XZ-2 from above, the lens at the front: the mode dial in yellow, the shutter button in orange with the zoom lever round it in light blue, ON/OFF in white](camera-top.svg)

At the back: the **screen**, which tilts, and which you can also touch; on
the right, the **arrow pad** with **OK** in the middle and the **sub dial**,
the ring round it that turns; above it the **playback** button, with the
triangle, and **Fn1**; below it **MENU** and **INFO**; and in the corner
the red **movie** button. At the front, round the lens, the **control
ring** turns too, and beside the lens are the **lever** and **Fn2**.

![The XZ-2 from behind: the screen, the arrow pad and sub dial in yellow with OK in white, the movie button in red, the flash switch in orange](camera-back.svg)

The battery and the card go in through a door underneath (manual, p. 5).
The battery charges in the camera, through the USB cable: the little lamp
is on while it charges and goes off when it is full, after up to five hours
(manual, p. 6). The first time it is switched on, the camera asks for its
language, the date and the time (manual, p. 7). **iAUTO** on the mode
dial is the fully automatic mode (manual, p. 8). The playback button shows
your pictures, and the sub dial goes from one to the next (manual, p. 11).
:::

## Keep trying

- Take five pictures of the same thing from five different places, then
  look at them one after another.
- On your camera, find the button with the lightning bolt and the button
  with the flower. If there is no flower, look in the MENU.
- Ask an adult what the first camera they ever used looked like.

::: adult
This chapter gives the vocabulary of the rest of the course (lens,
aperture, shutter, sensor, pixel, card) and gets the child to take a first
picture in the fully automatic mode, without settings. Please help with the
battery and the memory card the first time, and show where the strap goes:
round the wrist, always.

The "finding your way" list is there because most families will not have
the XZ-2. Any camera with a mode dial (P, A, S, M) can follow the whole
course; many phones have a manual or "pro" mode that does the same.
:::
