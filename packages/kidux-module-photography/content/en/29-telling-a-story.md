# Choosing, and telling a story

## One out of ten

Take ten pictures of something, and keep **one**: the one that says the
most with the least. Choosing is half of photography. The other nine are
not wasted: they are how you got to the one.

## Five pictures, one story

One picture shows a moment. Five, one after another, tell a **story**,
with a beginning, a middle and an end: a day at the beach, a walk to
school, a cake from the flour to the last crumb, the life of a
sandcastle.

![A sandcastle's story in five pictures: the bucket, the castle, a wave coming, the castle half gone, and flat sand with the flag lying on it](story-strip.svg)

> [cheer] Print your best picture, and put it on the wall. One photograph
> on a wall is worth more than a thousand on a screen.

## Ten projects to go on with

Photographers grow by working on one thing for a long time. Pick one:

1. **An alphabet of shapes**: find every letter, A to Z, in the world: a
   branch that is a Y, a gate that is an H.
2. **Shadows for a week**: one picture of a shadow every day.
3. **The same tree in four seasons**, from the same place.
4. **Every door in your street.**
5. **A portrait of each person at home**, doing what they like doing.
6. **Water in all its states**: ice, a drop, a puddle, steam, snow, a
   wave.
7. **A colour a day**: Monday red, Tuesday blue.
8. **The view from a window, every hour** of one day.
9. **Something tiny and something huge**, in pairs.
10. **Your town upside down**, in a box with a hole, as in chapter 1.

::: try
1. Choose one of the ten projects.
2. Take its first five pictures this week.
3. Choose the best of the five, and print it.
4. Make a story in five pictures of something you do every day.
:::

## Keep trying

- Show a friend your five-picture story without saying anything. Can they
  tell it back to you?
- Choose your best ten pictures of the year, and make a little book.
- Start a second project when the first one has fifty pictures.

::: adult
This chapter is about editing and sequencing: choosing one picture from
many, telling a story in a short series, and long projects, which are
how a child keeps photographing after the course ends.

Printing one picture a month, and putting it where the family sees it, is
the best encouragement there is.
:::
