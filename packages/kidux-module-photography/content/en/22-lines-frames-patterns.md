# Lines, frames and patterns

Before you lift the camera, look. The world is full of shapes that can
hold a picture together, if you put them where they help.

## Lines that lead

A road, a fence, a railway, the edge of a river: a line that starts near
the bottom of the picture and runs into it **takes the eye with it**, all
the way in. Stand where the line starts, low, and let it run towards what
you want people to look at.

![A road running straight away into the desert: the eye goes down it to the end](leading-lines.jpg)

## A frame inside the frame

A doorway, a window, an arch, two branches: look **through** something,
and it puts a frame round your picture, and the eye goes to what is
inside it.

![A house seen through a window: the window frames it](frame-in-frame.jpg)

## Patterns, and one that breaks them

Many of the same thing, tiles, windows, a row of boots, make a
**pattern**, calm and pleasing. But the picture often comes when **one**
thing breaks it: one red among the yellow, one shoe turned the wrong
way. The eye goes straight to it.

![A crowd of yellow tulips, and one red one](pattern-break.jpg)

## Mirrors on the ground

A puddle after the rain is a mirror lying on the ground. Get low, close
to it, and the trees and the sky appear upside down beneath the real
ones: **symmetry**, the same on both sides of a line.

![Trees and a bench reflected in a big puddle](reflection.jpg)

> [think] For symmetry, the rule of thirds can wait: put the line of
> the mirror right in the middle.

::: try
1. Find a path, a fence or a pavement. Crouch where it starts, and let it
   run into the picture.
2. Ask a friend to stand outside, and take them through an open door or
   a window, with the frame round them.
3. Find a pattern at home: shoes, books, cups, buttons. Take it. Then put
   one thing out of place, upside down or a different colour, and take it
   again.
4. After the rain, find a puddle, put the camera almost on the ground, and
   take what it reflects.
:::

::: camera
There is nothing to set: lines, frames and patterns are in the world, not
in the camera, and every camera can take them. The grid of chapter 21
helps to keep a line straight or a reflection level (manual, p. 60). The
screen tilts (manual, p. 3), so the camera can go down by the puddle
while you look from above.
:::

## Keep trying

- Look up in a tall building, or in a forest: lines that meet in the sky.
- Make a pattern of your own with toys or stones, and break it.
- Find a frame in nature: a branch, a gap in a hedge, a hole in a rock.

::: adult
This chapter is about seeing before photographing: leading lines,
frames within the frame, pattern and the element that breaks it, and
symmetry with reflections. None of it needs a setting; the habit of
looking around, and moving, before raising the camera is the lesson.
:::
